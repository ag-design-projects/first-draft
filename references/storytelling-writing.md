# Storytelling Writing Pass

Apply after choosing the argument and before rendering. The verified source for this revision is Ellen Lupton, *Design Is Storytelling*, “Clinic: Improve Your Writing”, printed pp. 154–155 (PDF pp. 156–157). These operational deck checks are adaptations.

- Replace vague categories with concrete people, actions, objects, and conditions when the evidence supports them.
- Prefer an identifiable subject and a precise verb. Use passive voice when the actor is unknown or the object deserves emphasis; do not invent an actor to avoid it.
- Replace inflated nouns with verbs where that improves meaning. Describe what changed rather than calling it “transformation” without explanation.
- Remove filler, repeated setup, and metaphors that make the reader decode the argument.
- Show evidence of a quality rather than merely naming it. “Easy to use” needs an observed behavior or a clearly labeled hypothesis.
- Read titles in sequence: they should communicate the argument without speaker notes. Make body copy support rather than repeat the title.
- Read speaker notes aloud. Revise awkward clauses and unclear references while retaining qualifications and necessary technical terms.
- Fit copy to layout capacity by simplifying or splitting the beat, not by deleting the evidence caveat or shrinking the type indefinitely.

Example written for this skill, not quoted from a book:

Weak: “The implementation of navigation optimization facilitated an enhancement in discoverability.”

Clearer, if supported: “Users found account settings faster after we renamed the menu.”

When results are unmeasured: “Test whether renaming the menu helps users find account settings.”
